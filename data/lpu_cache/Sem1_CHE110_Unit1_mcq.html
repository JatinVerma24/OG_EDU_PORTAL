1 What does the term environment refer to?

Introduction to environment Easy
A. Only the plants in a forest
B. Only the buildings around people
C. Only the weather of a place
D. The surroundings in which organisms live

Correct Answer: The surroundings in which organisms live

Explanation:

The environment includes all living and non-living surroundings that affect organisms.

Incorrect! Try again.

2 Which of the following is a natural component of the environment?

Introduction to environment Easy
A. Bridge
B. Air
C. Factory
D. Road

Correct Answer: Air

Explanation:

Air is a natural part of the environment, while roads, factories, and bridges are human-made.

Incorrect! Try again.

Components of environment Easy

B. Geological component

Correct Answer: Biotic component

23 A city replaces a wetland with buildings, after which flooding increases and bird populations decline. Which environmental components are most directly involved?

A. Biotic, abiotic, and cultural components
B. Abiotic and extraterrestrial components
C. Abiotic and cultural components only
D. Biotic and cultural components only

Correct Answer: Biotic, abiotic, and cultural components

Explanation:

Birds are biotic, water and soil are abiotic, and buildings and land-use decisions are cultural or human-made components.

24 Which observation best demonstrates an interaction between biotic and abiotic components of the environment?

Components of environment Medium
A. A city expands its public transport network
B. Two bird species compete for nesting space
C. Wind transports sand across an empty desert
D. Tree roots reduce soil erosion on a slope

Correct Answer: Tree roots reduce soil erosion on a slope

Explanation:

Tree roots are biotic, while soil is abiotic; the roots directly influence the stability of the soil.

25 Sulfur dioxide released into the air forms acid rain that lowers the pH of a lake and harms fish. Which sequence of Earth-sphere interactions is most accurate?

Spheres of earth Medium
A. Hydrosphere to atmosphere to lithosphere
B. Atmosphere to hydrosphere to biosphere
C. Biosphere to lithosphere to atmosphere
D. Lithosphere to biosphere to hydrosphere

Correct Answer: Atmosphere to hydrosphere to biosphere

Explanation:

The pollutant begins in the atmosphere, enters lake water in the hydrosphere, and then affects fish in the biosphere.

Spheres of earth Medium

Correct Answer: Lithosphere, hydrosphere, and biosphere

Explanation:

Soil belongs to the lithosphere, the river to the hydrosphere, and aquatic plants to the biosphere.

40 A government measures progress only by counting newly planted trees, without checking species diversity, survival rates, or restored habitat area. What is the main weakness of this SDG assessment?

41 A coastal city constructs a seawall to reduce storm-surge exposure. The wall protects urban property but accelerates erosion downstream, alters sediment transport, and encourages further construction in the protected zone. Which interpretation best reflects the environmental perspective?

B. The wall reduces total vulnerability whenever protected property values exceed construction costs
C. The wall creates only an economic externality because erosion remains a physical process
D. The wall eliminates environmental risk by separating society from coastal processes

Correct Answer: The wall converts a natural hazard into an interconnected socio-ecological risk

42 Two valleys receive the same annual rainfall and contain similar soils. One experiences severe flooding after forest conversion and settlement expansion, while the other does not. Which conclusion is most defensible?

Introduction to environment Hard
A. Similar soils imply that both valleys must have equal infiltration rates
B. Flooding should be classified as social rather than environmental in the settled valley
C. Environmental outcomes emerge from interactions among natural and human systems
D. Annual rainfall alone is sufficient to predict the environmental response

Correct Answer: Environmental outcomes emerge from interactions among natural and human systems

Explanation:

Land cover, drainage infrastructure, settlement patterns, and rainfall interact. Similar climatic and soil conditions do not guarantee identical environmental outcomes.

C. Abiotic to biotic to abiotic to social

45 Permafrost thaw releases methane, which strengthens atmospheric warming and causes additional thaw. Which Earth-sphere coupling directly initiates and then amplifies this feedback?

Spheres of earth Hard
A. Cryosphere-atmosphere coupling followed by atmospheric forcing of the cryosphere
B. Biosphere-hydrosphere coupling followed by hydrological forcing of the lithosphere
C. Atmosphere-biosphere coupling followed by biological forcing of the hydrosphere
D. Hydrosphere-lithosphere coupling followed by lithospheric forcing of the biosphere

Correct Answer: Cryosphere-atmosphere coupling followed by atmospheric forcing of the cryosphere

Explanation:

Frozen ground belongs to the cryosphere, while methane and warming operate through the atmosphere. Their two-way interaction produces a positive feedback.

46 A volcanic eruption injects sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere, forms sulfate aerosols, reduces incoming solar radiation, and temporarily lowers ocean-surface temperature. Which ordering best represents the dominant cross-sphere pathway?

Spheres of earth Hard
A. Atmosphere to lithosphere to biosphere
B. Hydrosphere to atmosphere to lithosphere
C. Lithosphere to atmosphere to hydrosphere
D. Biosphere to hydrosphere to atmosphere

Correct Answer: Lithosphere to atmosphere to hydrosphere

Explanation:

The eruption originates in the lithosphere, modifies atmospheric aerosol loading and radiation, and subsequently changes the temperature of the hydrosphere.

49 A region maintains constant total wealth by replacing a destroyed wetland with financial assets of equal estimated monetary value. The wetland had an irreplaceable flood-regulation threshold. Which sustainability interpretation rejects the substitution?

Concept of sustainability Hard
A. Strong sustainability because critical natural capital must be maintained
B. Fiscal sustainability because financial assets cannot fund ecosystem restoration
C. Procedural sustainability because consultation determines ecological equivalence
D. Weak sustainability because aggregate capital must remain constant

Correct Answer: Strong sustainability because critical natural capital must be maintained

Explanation:

Strong sustainability limits substitution when natural capital performs critical or irreversible functions. Equal monetary value does not guarantee ecological equivalence.

Sustainability concerns population structure, genetic diversity, and resilience as well as total abundance and harvest rates.

51 A government subsidizes efficient air conditioners. Electricity used per unit falls by 30%, but lower operating costs increase ownership and usage enough that total electricity demand rises. Which policy conclusion best follows?

Sustainable development Hard
A. The increase proves that efficient technology has no environmental value
B. Efficiency standards should be paired with measures addressing total consumption
C. Rising demand is sustainable whenever individual devices consume less electricity
D. Efficiency improvements necessarily reduce aggregate resource use over time

Correct Answer: Efficiency standards should be paired with measures addressing total consumption

Explanation:

This is a rebound effect: efficiency lowers service cost and can increase total use. Sustainable development policy must address both efficiency and aggregate demand.
