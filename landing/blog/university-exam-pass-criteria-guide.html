Understanding University Exam Passing Criteria: End-Term Minimums vs Overall Aggregates

Every semester, thousands of students scoring strong continuous internal assessment marks still receive unexpected backlogs because they fail the End-Term minimum qualifying threshold. Here is how modern university passing rules actually work.

1. The Two-Tier Passing Architecture

To pass an academic course, university examination guidelines mandate that a student satisfy two independent conditions simultaneously:

The Dual Criteria Rule
$$\text{Rule 1: } \text{ETE Score} \ge 30\% \quad \text{OR} \quad (\text{MTE} + \text{ETE Score}) \ge 30\%$$
$$\text{Rule 2: } \text{Total Aggregate Score} \ge 40\%$$

Failing either one of these conditions results in an 'F' (Fail) or 'E' (Re-appear) grade, regardless of how high your marks are in the other component.

2. Continuous Assessment vs End-Term Weightage

A typical 100-mark university course is structured as follows:

  • Continuous Assessment (CA) — 30 to 40 Marks: Quizzes, homework assignments, class participation, and mid-term tests.
  • Mid-Term Examination (MTE) — 20 to 25 Marks: Mid-semester subjective test.
  • End-Term Examination (ETE) — 40 to 50 Marks: Comprehensive final examination evaluating the entire syllabus.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

If I get a re-appear in ETE, do I have to repeat my continuous assessments?

In most university frameworks, your internal CA marks are preserved. When you write the re-appear examination, your new ETE score is combined with your original internal marks.
