How TGPA and SGPA Are Calculated: Grade Points, Letter Grades & Credit Multipliers

Term Grade Point Average (TGPA)—also referred to as Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)—reflects a student's academic standing during an individual semester. Learn how letter grades map to numerical grade points and how course credits determine your final semester standing.

1. What is TGPA / SGPA?

While CGPA measures your cumulative record across your entire degree, TGPA/SGPA measures your performance strictly within a single semester. It forms the atomic building block from which your overall CGPA is derived.

2. The UGC 10-Point Letter Grade Scale

The University Grants Commission recommends a standardized 10-point absolute or relative grading scale. Each letter grade corresponds to a specific numerical grade point:

3. Step-by-Step Calculation Method

To calculate your TGPA for any semester:

  1. List each registered subject along with its allotted course credits ($C_j$).
  2. Obtain the numerical grade point ($G_j$) corresponding to the letter grade earned.
  3. Multiply the credits by the grade point to calculate Credit Points ($CP_j = C_j \times G_j$).
  4. Sum the credit points across all subjects: $\sum CP_j$.
  5. Divide by the total registered credits for the semester: $\sum C_j$.
TGPA / SGPA Equation
$$\text{TGPA} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m} (C_j \times G_j)}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} C_j}$$

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Is relative grading better than absolute grading?

In tough engineering courses, relative grading adjusts cutoffs according to batch performance, ensuring that an abnormally difficult paper does not artificially depress the entire cohort's grades.

What happens if I get an 'F' in a 1-credit lab vs a 4-credit theory course?

Failing a 4-credit course costs you up to 40 credit points, which severely damages your TGPA. Failing a 1-credit lab costs at most 10 credit points, though both will require a re-appear exam.
